About Kendo

The Way of the Sword

01

What is Kendo?

Kendo (剣道) is a modern Japanese martial art that evolved from traditional samurai swordsmanship (kenjutsu). The name means The Way of the Sword — a discipline that uses bamboo swords (shinai) and protective armour (bogu) to practise techniques rooted in centuries of sword culture.

Unlike sport fencing, kendo is as much about forging character as it is about technical skill. Practitioners, known as kendoka, train to cultivate focus, perseverance, courtesy, and respect — values that carry well beyond the dojo.

02

Philosophy of Kendo

The All Japan Kendo Federation defines the purpose of kendo as "disciplining the human character through the application of the principles of the katana." Central to this is the concept of Ken Zen Ichi Nyo (剣禅一如) — the unity of sword and Zen — meaning that physical practice and mental refinement are inseparable.

Every aspect of training, from the bow before entering the dojo to the controlled aggression of a strike, reflects core values:

  • Rei (礼) — Etiquette and respect for opponents, instructors, and the art itself.
  • Ki (気) — Spirit and fighting intent, expressed through the kiai (shout).
  • Ken (剣) — Correct technique and the proper use of the sword.
  • Tai (体) — Posture, footwork, and body movement working in harmony.
"The concept and purpose of Kendo is to discipline the human character through the application of the principles of the katana." — All Japan Kendo Federation
03

Equipment

Kendo requires specialised gear. Beginners typically start with just a shinai and gradually add armour as they advance. A full set of bogu is a significant investment but lasts many years with proper care.

Shinai 竹刀

A sword made of four bamboo staves bound together, used for all sparring and drills. Standard adult shinai are 114 cm (men) or 112 cm (women).

Bokken 木刀

A solid wooden sword used for kata (formal exercises). Bokken practice develops correct form and an understanding of cutting mechanics.

Men 面

The helmet that protects the head, face, and throat. It is the most recognisable piece of kendo armour and one of the four valid target areas.

Kote 小手

Thick gauntlets that protect the wrists and forearms — another valid striking target. They allow enough dexterity to grip and swing the shinai freely.

Do 胴

A cuirass of lacquered bamboo or plastic that protects the torso and sides. A strike to the right side of the do is a valid scoring target.

Gi & Hakama 着物・袴

The traditional uniform: a thick cotton jacket (gi) and wide pleated trousers (hakama). The five pleats of the hakama represent kendo's core virtues.

04

Competitions

Kendo competitions (shiai) are held at club, regional, national, and international levels. Matches are decided by a panel of referees who award points (ippon) for strikes that demonstrate correct form, spirit, and follow-through simultaneously.

Individual Matches

Most tournaments feature individual brackets. Matches are typically 3–5 minutes long. The first competitor to score two ippons wins; a single ippon decides if time expires.

Team Matches

Teams of three or five compete in fixed order. The team with more individual wins advances; total ippons are used as a tiebreaker. University team events are a highlight of the calendar.

Scoring Targets

Valid targets are men (head), kote (right wrist), do (right torso), and tsuki (throat thrust — advanced ranks only). Each must be accompanied by a kiai and correct zanshin (remaining posture).

05

Gradings & Progression

Kendo uses a two-tier ranking system of kyu (student grades) and dan (black-belt grades). Grading examinations test technique, etiquette, and knowledge of kendo principles. Progress is steady — a typical path from beginner to first dan takes two to three years of consistent training.

Rank Level Notes
6-1 Kyu Beginner Foundational strikes, footwork, and basic etiquette. No bogu required for early kyu grades.
1 Dan (Shodan) Intermediate First black-belt grade. Requires consistent technique and demonstrated spirit in shiai.
2-4 Dan Advanced Deepening refinement of technique, kata proficiency, and understanding of kendo principles.
5-8 Dan Expert Reserved for experienced practitioners. 8 Dan is the highest rank — fewer than 200 people hold it worldwide.
Renshi / Kyoshi / Hanshi Teaching Titles Honorary titles awarded alongside high dan grades, recognising outstanding teaching and contribution to kendo.
